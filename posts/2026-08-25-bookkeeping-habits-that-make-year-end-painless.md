Every March, a familiar scene plays out: boxes of receipts, unreconciled accounts, unexplained balances, and a finance team working late to reconstruct a year that was never recorded properly in the first place. The remedy is not a heroic effort at year-end. It is a set of small habits practised every week.

## 1. Record transactions as they happen

The single most valuable habit. Entries made within a day or two of the event are accurate because the details are fresh. Entries made months later are reconstructed, and reconstruction is where errors are born. Set a fixed rhythm: daily for cash and bank, weekly for everything else.

## 2. Reconcile the bank every month, without exception

A bank reconciliation is the honesty check for the whole ledger. If it is done monthly, differences are small and easy to trace. If it is left for the year end, the differences compound into a puzzle that takes days to solve. Treat the monthly reconciliation as non-negotiable.

## 3. Keep every document, in one place, in a predictable order

Digital or physical does not matter. What matters is that a document can be found in a minute, by anyone, without asking. A simple structure works: one folder per month, files named by date and party. Photograph paper bills on the day they arrive.

## 4. Match the GST records to the books every month

Reconciling the GST returns with the sales and purchase registers each month keeps input credit clean and prevents the year-end discovery of mismatches that have been sitting unnoticed for months. Fixing a difference in the month it occurred is easy; fixing it a year later often is not.

## 5. Chase receivables on a schedule

A receivables ageing report reviewed every fortnight, with follow-up on anything overdue, keeps cash flowing and prevents old balances from silently becoming bad debts. It also keeps the year-end provisioning discussion short, because there are no surprises.

## 6. Separate business and personal money completely

Mixed accounts create mixed records. A dedicated business bank account, a business card, and a clear rule that personal expenses never touch the business ledger will save more reconciliation effort than almost anything else.

## 7. Review a short set of numbers every month

Owners do not need a full set of accounts each month, but they should look at a few figures consistently: cash balance, receivables and payables, sales against the previous month, and any expense that looks out of pattern. This review catches errors while they are cheap and keeps the books honest simply because someone is looking.

## The result

Businesses that keep these habits arrive at year-end with books that are already close to final. The audit is shorter, the tax filings are accurate, the numbers can be trusted for decisions, and March looks like any other month.
